# The AUKORA Golden Boundary, v2: full sovereignty

Long-form white paper behind this charter: [AUKORA Golden Boundary, Sovereign edition](https://github.com/aumara-xyz/aukora-sovereign/blob/main/docs/GOLDEN-BOUNDARY-PAPER.md).

> The Golden Boundary is the line between what anything may propose and what only the person may permit: no model,
> message, record, server or builder, AUMARA included, can supply the owner's approval, and none can take away the
> owner's identity, keys, memory, node or way out.

Adopted 16 October 2026. AUKORA Sovereign is the node you control; AUKORA Light is the everyday messenger. They share
one identity, one set of formats and this charter: both repositories carry this same file, byte for byte, and a change
lands in both. Each principle is a rule, then **Sovereign:** (the organ or plan rows that enforce it) and
**Light:** (the brick or rule that honours it).

## How to read this
- **NOW** means scheduled in a milestone (Sovereign M1 to M8, Light M1 to M5). **LATER** means a "Later" row or no plan
  at all; "not yet in the plan" says which, and the Gaps collect them. A heading says NOW when a milestone delivers the
  core of the rule; the lines under it name what waits. A principle stays a promise until its hostile test passes on the
  installed node (Sovereign) or on main, deployed (Light).
- This distils Prime's Golden Boundary white paper (1 October 2026), the research page (rev 14), the Unownable Core
  (draft) and the concepts, not the paths or fixes, of the exit-architecture spec. Phone, firmware, board and project
  names in that spec are unverified candidates; nothing here depends on them.
- The ceiling: "unownable" is a target, not a fact. Open source does not abolish law, coercion, hosting, domains,
  signing keys or governance capture. A passkey approval shows that a passkey signed exact bytes, not who pressed it.

## Part I. The core: what may never cross the boundary
### 1. Capability does not create authority. (NOW)
A model, app or agent may become as capable as it likes. What it may do changes only by an act the owner signs. The part
that proposes is never the part that permits, and that includes changes to itself.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Gate and Aura (33, 35); the cage (26); the supervisor as sole reader of FD 3 (128); the model key
  outside the model (62); a new model starts with no grants (138); self-change through the gate (14, 64).
- **Light:** NOW. Auma never acts: she reads what you enabled and drafts, and you send (rule 6; bricks 9, 10). Heavier
  work goes to a paired node's approval card (brick 16).

### 2. Evidence, identity, messages and presence never become permission. (NOW)
Scores, flags, pictures, receipts, relay agreement, model confidence, a valid signature, a vouch, an invitation, a known
sender, nearness between devices: each may refuse, escalate or inform. Only the owner's passkey, or a bounded policy the
owner pre-signed, permits. Ruling out the alternatives does not make the last one legitimate.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Constitution 3; Laya escalates and never approves (36, 37); no effect path reads the health map or
  a picture (55, 116); inbound text is data (135); "met in person" is evidence only (137).
- **Light:** NOW. An invitation is an introduction, never proof of a unique human (rule 1); inferences are labelled
  suggestions (rule 4). Brick 12 must not rest on a by-eye picture match (L5).

### 3. Permission is for exact bytes, once, and unknown stays unknown. (NOW)
The owner signs the operation itself, not a description the proposer wrote. A permission is used once, expires, and
stays used after a restart, a restore or a copied disk. Interrupted work is "unknown", never success and never fresh
permission. "Approved" is not "still wanted", and "published" is not "delivered".
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Exact bytes, consumed once, crash-injected (33, 34); three-valued decisions (40); epoch, sequence
  and previous hash on every authority record (110); spent approvals never revive (65). LATER: the clone guard (111),
  waiting on decision 4; until then a cloned disk is not defended.
- **Light:** NOW. Delivery shows pending, relayed or delivered, never a guess (rule 7; brick 6); a node's approval is
  over exact bytes, and the node verifies it itself (brick 16).

### 4. Guards fail closed, and check floors only grow. (NOW)
A guard sits on the only route to its effect. If it is disabled, removed or crashes, the effect stops; nothing the actor
can switch off may leave the door open. Fixed checks never shrink, adaptive layers only add, and a separate process
hunts for hazards nobody wrote a check for.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Constitution 4; a supervisor that only wires (128); every authority entry point and effect route
  has one terminal row (41); the antibody ratchet with fresh held-out cases (66); thymus, MHC, hypermutation (67); an
  unwritten check fails (115).
- **Light:** NOW. Strict CSP, with no eval, innerHTML or inline handlers (rule 8; brick 1); a members-only relay (brick
  5); contact rules run on the client because a gift wrap hides the sender.

### 5. Repetition does not create evidence. (NOW)
Ten copies of one claim are one claim. A record keeps who said it, when, and what contradicts it. Disagreement and
corrections are kept, not averaged away. A summary is never the only copy, and an unavailable store is not an empty one.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Kira's cited recall answers found, empty or undetermined, and usage never raises confidence (57).
  LATER: anti-self-consensus (96).
- **Light:** NOW. Messages are the source; each Auma item links to them; corrections are kept; Auma says "I couldn't
  find a reply in the messages stored here", never "you ignored them" (rule 4; bricks 9, 10).

### 6. Collaboration does not merge sovereignty. (NOW)
A message, a proposal and an authorized effect are three things, even on one wire. An invitation, a task or a vouch is
never a grant over someone else's machine. Delegation only narrows, and new identities cannot multiply allowances.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Network and Gate: conserved invite allowances (72); constitution 5; delegation lineage (48); a
  closed grant list for apps (120); strangers refused before the agent sees anything (135). LATER: vouching (91).
- **Light:** NOW for 1:1: contact-first, invitation-only, nothing public (rule 1; bricks 3, 4). LATER: MLS groups.

### 7. A change of form is not a change of meaning. (NOW)
An old receipt, grant or permission never means more under a new schema, model or screen. Unknown message versions are
refused, not repaired, and every format carries an algorithm id. A change to the boundary names its predecessor, is
versioned and diffable, never widens an old grant, and can be declined.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Canon (32); frozen contracts where unknown operations refuse (wave 0); crypto agility (139);
  epochs (110). Not yet in the plan: how this charter is amended, and by whom once the founder steps back (G1, G9).
- **Light:** NOW. Shared formats are pinned by hash; vectors are copied and code never is (rule 2).

### 8. The boundary stays useful, and refusing stays cheap. (NOW)
A door that never opens is not safety: authorized work must pass at a cost the owner can bear, and false refusals are
measured with what was blocked. Saying no, pausing or leaving is cheap and never held against the person. A model's
words about its own proposal are fenced as unverified; persuasion may reach the person, never the effect.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. The Laya slider (36); declined proposals are remembered (112); a triage benchmark counts owner
  time and false alarms (37). Not yet in the plan: a test that owners can restate what they approved (G8).
- **Light:** NOW. Usable offline and with no model (rule 7); no ceremony per message; every failure case is
  understandable and correctable (section 6).

## Part II. The Unownable Core: what nobody can take
### 9. Identity, keys, memory and node belong to the person. (NOW)
Nobody (AUMARA, its founder, a host, an inviter, a guardian, a model provider) can own, seize, revoke or lock out a
person's identity, keys, memory or node. The owner root is never under anyone else's control. A key shows control of a
key, not a unique human. A body is never the key: a biometric only unlocks a passkey, and a path without it exists.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. AUMLOK: constitution 1; the key hierarchy with scopes and epochs (113); passkeys (43); the phrase
  seals a random root (44); a stranger cannot lock the owner out (49). LATER: guardian modes (125).
- **Light:** NOW for keys: derived on the person's device from a random root the phrase seals (brick 2; rule 2); an inviter never
  owns or destroys the invitee's identity (rule 1). Not yet in the plan: relay and admission sit under the founder's
  Cloudflare login (rule 9), so access to Light is not yet unownable (L1).

### 10. No hidden ruler, the founder included. (LATER)
The founder and builders occupy roles; they do not own the protocol. Benevolence is not a security primitive: for every
part, ask what its operator could force if coerced, compromised, absent or dead, and make the answer shrink. Succession
is a rule with a quorum and a log, never a person. Hosts, firms, nations and models may serve the protocol; none is
needed for its continuity, and money never buys authority over a person or the core.
- **Sovereign:** LATER. The five organs split the roles. Succession by k of n and witness committees are row 89; token
  and economics research (97) must obey the money rule. Not yet in the plan: a list of what the founder's keys can
  change on anyone else's node, and a test of "founder absent, coerced or compromised" (G1).
- **Light:** LATER. Not yet in the plan; the founder's login is the operator seat (rule 9; L1).

### 11. Open, rebuildable, forkable. (NOW)
Code, specs and verification rules are open (AGPL-3.0 in both repositories); anyone can run, rebuild and fork them, and
a running binary must match a build anyone can reproduce. Open source is necessary and not enough: domains, signing
keys, release channels and the name are control points, each kept replaceable. Owners may refuse an update, or fork.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Body: reproducible build, byte-compared with the deployed binary (81); release floor (65);
  publication waits until every major AI has attacked main. LATER: conformance corpus (95), licence questions (99). Not
  yet in the plan: an independent second rebuild and owner-chosen release signers (G7).
- **Light:** NOW for the licence. The web client is served by the operator's host (section 4), which could swap the code
  that holds the vault. Not yet in the plan: a pinned, reproducible bundle the browser checks (L2).

### 12. Exit is always possible, and rehearsed. (NOW)
A person can leave AUKORA, or change model, device, host or relay, keep identity, memory, receipts and relationships,
and verify them offline without the old provider's help. The exit is tested before it is claimed: replace a model, move
the node, change a relay, check the history.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Member exit: export keys, memory and receipts, verify offline, restore with original ids (61);
  cold proof export and the stranger's verifier (51, 52); the move off S1 by that path (103).
- **Light:** NOW in part. Export and import in formats Sovereign's Kira reads (brick 17); messages are standard NIP-17,
  tested against other clients (brick 15). Not yet in the plan: an exit that needs no Sovereign node (L4).

### 13. Memory is the person's; each use is its own permission. (NOW)
Remember, store, export, train, publish and act are separate permissions, and memory is encrypted under keys the person
holds. Forgetting says what it did: destroying a key makes a local copy unreadable; demoting retires a record but keeps
it. Neither erases copies others hold. What comes in is data with its source, never an instruction.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Kira demotes and never deletes (57); permissions to disclose, store, dataset, export, train and
  activate (59); inbound is data (135). Not yet in the plan: an owner-ordered erase by key destruction (G5).
- **Light:** NOW. A switch per conversation; memory encrypted on the device; forgetting destroys keys (rule 5; brick 8);
  a cloud model only with per-conversation consent (section 4). Light destroys keys where Sovereign demotes (G5).

## Part III. The exit layers: how sovereignty survives loss
### 14. Four tiers, each swappable. (NOW)
(1) A pocket vault: a phone or key that holds the passkey and approves. (2) A computer the person controls, with
inspectable firmware, for the node. (3) A home AI box for models, on the person's hardware. (4) A mirror holding only
ciphertext. Only tier 1 approves. A tier can be lost, replaced or hosted by someone else without handing them authority.
- **Sovereign:** Tier 1 NOW: passkeys (43), a hardware-held root on several devices (113), the phone layout (107);
  LATER: NIP-46 phone approval, a phone app (87, 90). Tiers 2 and 3 NOW as a Linux node on LXD or a Nebius VM (73),
  readable by its host until confidential VMs (88, LATER); LATER: local models (86). Not yet in the plan: running on the
  person's own hardware as a supported path, and reaching a home box with no public address (G2).
- **Light:** Tier 1 NOW is Light's home: the passkey vault in a phone or browser (bricks 2, 7, 13). Tier 3: the model
  slot may be your Sovereign node (bricks 11, 16). Tier 4: see 16.

### 15. Recovery without surrender. (NOW)
If every device is lost, the person gets back identity, memory and node from what they hold or chose: the phrase, a
protected backup, guardians they picked. The exit spec's 60-second fresh-phone re-bind is an unmeasured target. Recovery
advances the key epoch, evicts the lost device, and restores memory but never spent permissions. Guardians help with an
operational key; replacing the owner root is a separate mode only the owner can choose. Silence is never consent.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. AUMLOK: seven-word phrase and refresh (44, 45); passkey-bound backup (46); epochs (110); restore
  (61). LATER: guardians with 2q - n > f and rollback-protected vote locks (125); watchtowers, veto and notice (126);
  clone guard (111); decisions 1 to 3. Not yet in the plan: a lose-everything drill (G3).
- **Light:** NOW. One recovery phrase restores the same person in both products (rule 2; brick 2). LATER: guardian
  recovery. NOW in part: the recovery file and the seven words restore the identity, and the relay returns the last
  30 days of gift wraps, the person's own copies included. Not yet: anything older than 30 days (L4).

### 16. A mirror that holds only ciphertext. (NOW)
A mirror (a relay, an object store, a second node, a friend's box) may keep the person's encrypted memory, record heads
and signed releases, so losing every device is not losing a life. It never holds a key or readable content, and anyone
can run one, AUMARA included, with no more standing than anyone else. A mirror that withholds or rolls back is caught by
witnesses kept elsewhere, and what it can still learn (sizes, timing, who talks to whom) is stated.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Network and Kira: heads witnessed on public relays (54); encrypted memory backup on relays and
  between the owner's nodes (60); owner-browser witness (53); cold export (51). LATER: self-healing backups (124). Not
  yet in the plan: the Aura chain and signed bundles on a mirror, a lying-mirror test, what a mirror learns (G4).
- **Light:** NOW. The relay holds only NIP-17 gift wraps; the admission service never sees a message or key (bricks 3,
  5). Not yet in the plan: a mirror of Light's own store (L4).

### 17. A device swarm the owner controls. (LATER)
A person's devices form one group. Adding or removing a device is an owner act with a passkey receipt; it advances the
key epoch and the group's encryption epoch, so a removed or stolen device reads nothing new. The group channel (MLS over
Nostr is a candidate to evaluate) carries traffic and never decides what is legitimate. Being near is evidence, not
authority.
- **Sovereign:** Device keys, pairing by co-presence and delegation lineage are NOW (47, 48, 113). LATER: MLS group
  rooms (123), Bluetooth co-presence and mesh (132, 134), bitchat adapter (133). Not yet in the plan: the rule that a
  membership change needs an owner receipt, and a stolen-device eviction drill (G3).
- **Light:** LATER. Same identity on web and native and a Bluetooth mesh come in M4 (bricks 13, 14); groups over MLS are
  Later. Not yet in the plan: a key per device, and revoking one (L3).

### 18. Capability cells. (NOW)
Anything new that runs for the person (a skill, tool, app or self-change) arrives as a small, signed, hash-pinned unit
with a declared list of what it may touch. The approval card shows that list in plain words, checked by the runtime and
not taken from the author's note, so the owner sees "no network, no files" before signing.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Body: the 101-byte WASM cell, digests bound into the authorization (64); apps as signed plugins
  with a closed grant list, caged, no network, exec or WASI (120, 121). Not yet in the plan: a runtime-checked label on
  the card; rows 64 and 120 keep apps out of the cell, so cells as the app format is Peter's call (G6).
- **Light:** NOW, by absence: nothing from outside runs (rule 8); Auma never acts. Later skills would use the same
  format.

## Part IV. How claims stay honest
### 19. Strangers can check, and every claim carries its ceiling. (NOW)
Anyone who does not trust AUKORA can check a claim of record from an empty folder, against heads kept elsewhere, and
each claim says what it cannot show. Records are private by default, disclosed in the smallest piece that answers the
question. A spec is not enforcement, a file or a passing test is not acceptance, and "unmeasured" is an honest result.
- **Sovereign:** NOW. Aura, cold export, verifier and witnesses (50 to 54); an unconfined control for each attack (29);
  a hostile twin at every milestone; the plan's "Say plainly" list. Each principle still needs its named test (G9).
- **Light:** NOW. The acceptance journey and its failure cases (section 6); the "to verify" list (section 7); Aura keeps
  a quiet local history and publishes nothing.

## Gaps: what the boundary requires that neither plan covers yet
Every gap is LATER until Peter schedules it. None is a row; each goes to the IDEAS list as "switch target or park it?".

**Sovereign**
- **G1. Founder-force inventory and test.** The Unownable Core (section 13) asks for a published "founder absent,
  coerced or compromised" test before an identity binding counts as a milestone; the plan binds in M1 and has succession
  only as row 89. Needed: a list of what the founder's keys can change on anyone else's node, shrinking over time.
- **G2. Hosting and reach.** Spawn uses LXD and Nebius VMs (73); a host can read a running node until row 88. Needed: a
  disclosure at Go Sovereign (74), a supported self-hosting path, and reach for a home box with no public address.
- **G3. Loss drills and heirs.** Lose everything (new device, old one evicted, memory back, spent stays spent) or lose
  one device (evicted, keys rotated), both measured; 60 seconds is a target. Unplanned: an heir path for death or
  incapacity.
- **G4. Mirror completeness.** The Aura chain and signed bundles on the mirror, a lying or rolling-back mirror detected,
  and what a mirror learns stated.
- **G5. Owner-ordered erase** by key destruction, apart from demotion, with one meaning of "forget" for both products.
- **G6. A runtime-checked capability label** on the approval card, and Peter's choice between cells and cages for apps.
- **G7. Release-source independence.** Second-party rebuilds, owners approving releases from a signer they choose, and
  custody of the name and domains.
- **G8. Coercion and understanding.** A signature can be forced or given blind. Needed: a duress path (a delay, a second
  person or a panic wipe) and a test that owners can restate what they approved.
- **G9. This charter.** Adoption, a test per principle, its hash in releases, and an amendment rule past the founder.

**Light**
- **L1. Operator independence.** Relay, admission and the web client sit under one Cloudflare login. Needed: a tested
  move to another relay or host, and contacts and history that keep working without the admission service.
- **L2. Web integrity:** a pinned, reproducible bundle the browser checks, so a host cannot swap the vault's code.
- **L3. A key per device,** revocation, and a device-list epoch.
- **L4. Full restore after a lost phone** (today the relay returns the last 30 days), and an export in an open format
  that needs no Sovereign node.
- **L5. Co-presence:** a by-eye picture match is never the security check; use the signed exchange or exact codes.
